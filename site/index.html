A new doorway into Dorrigo's stories

Begin at the museum. Discover the town.

The museum sits on a prominent approach to Dorrigo. An interactive display at its entrance could invite visitors to explore local stories, then follow them to historic places around town.

The opportunity

One place to start. Many stories to follow.

Historic logging tracks in the surrounding forests offer a starting point for exploring the area's working past. The museum can connect those histories and help people encounter the township as well as the national park.

The planned route of NSW's Dorrigo Escarpment Great Walk includes sections of historic logging and snig tracks. This offers a direct connection between the area's working past and present-day visitor experiences. NSW project information.

How a visit could unfold

From a screen to the street.

A shared set of museum-curated stories would appear on the display, on a phone and on the web. The first trail could start with just a few places whose owners and histories are ready.

01

Meet the gateway

At the museum, choose a short story, check opening information or explore a walking route, even outside volunteer hours.

02

Follow a place

A QR marker at an agreed historic site opens a concise story with photographs, sources and a suggestion for the next stop.

03

Return to the collection

The website and museum connect each place to objects, records and the people who know them. New accessions enrich the trail over time.

This demonstration shows what a visitor might see after scanning a plaque outside the Heritage Hotel Dorrigo. Its historical facts are sourced; the design and trail are proposals awaiting museum and site-owner review.

Demonstration story · stop 01

The hotel at the heart of town

Built in 1925 by Michael Feros, the hotel was added to the NSW State Heritage Register in 2026. Its story links migration, family stewardship and Dorrigo's social life.

A staged museum project

Build the foundation, then let the trail grow.

The first stage would provide a weather-rated display, website, a small QR pilot, digitisation equipment and museum-controlled collection data. Volunteers would approve what is published; supervised students could help prepare priority records and draft stories.

This is a concept for committee discussion and a possible grant application. No plaques or display have been installed.
